Chase CSV to QuickBooks Online

Plain steps to get your Chase CSV export into QuickBooks Online through Banking > Upload from file. The fixing happens in your browser; the file is not uploaded anywhere.

Open the tool with the Chase preset

1. Download the CSV from Chase

  1. Sign in at chase.com on a computer (the mobile app has fewer export options).
  2. Open the checking, savings or credit card account and go to its Activity view.
  3. Click the download icon above the transaction list.
  4. Pick the date range and choose Spreadsheet (CSV) as the file type, then download.
  5. Chase only offers recent activity as a download. Older months exist as PDF statements, which this tool does not read.

Bank menus change from time to time. If a label differs, search the Chase help pages for "download transactions" or "export CSV".

2. What is messy about the Chase file

  • Checking and credit card files have different headers. Checking: Details, Posting Date, Description, Amount, Type, Balance, Check or Slip #. Card: Transaction Date, Post Date, Description, Category, Type, Amount (some files add Memo).
  • Most of those columns (Details, Type, Balance, Category, Check or Slip #) mean nothing to the QuickBooks Online importer and get in the way of column mapping.
  • Amount is already signed (money out negative), which is what QuickBooks wants, but the file still needs trimming to Date, Description, Amount.
  • If your file was edited by hand or comes from another bank with Beginning balance / Ending balance rows and Debit/Credit columns with parentheses, use the Generic bank (Chase-like) preset instead. It strips those rows and checks the balance math.

3. How QBO CSV Fixer fixes it

  1. Open QBO CSV Fixer with the Chase preset and drop your CSV on the page. The preset is also auto-detected from the file layout.
  2. Check the preview, the reconcile badge and, if shown, the date format warning.
  3. Download the QuickBooks-ready CSV.

What the Chase preset does:

  • Recognizes both Chase layouts from the header and picks Posting Date (checking) or Transaction Date (card).
  • Keeps Description and the signed Amount, drops the other columns.
  • Writes dates as MM/DD/YYYY and offers the 3-column (Date, Description, Amount) or 4-column (Date, Description, Debit, Credit) QuickBooks layout.

Want to try it first? Download the Chase sample CSV (fake merchants) and drop it on the tool.

4. Upload to QuickBooks Online

  1. In QuickBooks Online, open Banking (in newer menus: Transactions, then Bank transactions).
  2. Select the account, open the Link account drop-down and choose Upload from file.
  3. Drag in the CSV you downloaded from QBO CSV Fixer and pick the QuickBooks account it belongs to.
  4. Map the columns: Date to Date, Description to Description, and Amount as one column with positive and negative values (or Debit and Credit as two columns if you exported the 4-column layout). Choose the MM/dd/yyyy date format.
  5. In the free export each description ends with "(QBO CSV Fixer free)" and the file name ends with _qbo-csv-fixer-free. There is no extra watermark row to remove.
  6. Select the transactions to import, then Continue and Done. They land in For review to categorize or match.

Good to know

  • Chase files carry no opening or closing balance row, so the reconcile badge shows N/A. Compare the net total against your statement before importing.

Scope

CSV only. No PDF statements, no bank login, no server: the file is read and converted by JavaScript in your browser. Free core: 1 file, up to 100 rows, watermarked export. Unlimited version coming soon.
